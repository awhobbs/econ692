\section{Triaging Reviewer Comments}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{frame}{Not All Comments Deserve Equal Weight}
  You have limited time. Reviewers will flag more things than you can fix. \textbf{\textcolor{usfgreen}{Triage before you revise.}}

  \vspace{8pt}

  Sort every comment into one of three buckets:

  \vspace{6pt}

  \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
    \item \textbf{Substantive.} The comment identifies a real flaw a reader will notice --- unclear argument, missing control, weak identification story, confusing figure.
    \item \textbf{Cosmetic.} Typos, wording, formatting. Easy to fix. Batch these.
    \item \textbf{Ignore (with reason).} Reviewer misunderstood, out of scope, or you disagree on substance. Write down \textit{why} you're ignoring it.
  \end{enumerate}

  \vspace{8pt}

  You don't have to accept every suggestion. But you do have to engage with each one.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{When Reviewers Disagree}
  It will happen. Two reviewers contradict each other, or one says ``cut this section'' and the other says ``expand it.''

  \vspace{8pt}

  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
    \item Both being confused about the same thing --- even in different ways --- usually means \textbf{the writing is the problem}, not their reading.
    \item If only one reviewer raised a concern, weight it by how central the issue is.
    \item When in doubt, go with what makes the argument clearer, not what makes the paper longer.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{8pt}

  \begin{block}{Heuristic}
    If you can't decide whether to make a change, make it. Revision is cheap; a confused reader at the final submission is not.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Pick Your One Biggest Issue}
  Before the sprint starts, write down the \textbf{single biggest weakness} in your draft. Not a list. One thing.

  \vspace{8pt}

  Candidates:

  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
    \item The introduction doesn't make the research question clear
    \item The identification story has a hole
    \item The main figure doesn't show what I claim it shows
    \item The results section reports numbers without interpretation
    \item The conclusion restates the intro and adds nothing
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{8pt}

  Sprint 1 (60 min) is for fixing \textit{that} thing. Everything else waits.
\end{frame}

\section{Polishing Pass}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{frame}{What a Polishing Pass Actually Is}
  Polishing $\ne$ proofreading. Proofreading catches typos; polishing catches \textbf{places where the reader has to work too hard.}

  \vspace{8pt}

  Read your paper out loud. When you stumble, the sentence is broken. Common stumbles:

  \vspace{6pt}

  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
    \item Long sentences with multiple clauses
    \item Pronouns with unclear referents (``this'' --- this what?)
    \item Acronyms introduced too late, or never
    \item Passive voice hiding who did what
    \item Paragraphs where the first sentence doesn't tell you what it's about
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The Introduction Is the Only Section That Matters}
  That's an overstatement, but not by much. Most readers read the intro and skim the rest.

  \vspace{8pt}

  \textbf{Your intro should answer, in this order:}

  \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
    \item What's the question?
    \item Why is it hard or unresolved?
    \item What do you do differently?
    \item What do you find?
    \item Why should the reader care?
  \end{enumerate}

  \vspace{8pt}

  If any of these is missing or buried past paragraph 3, fix it. The intro is where revision effort pays off most.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Tables and Figures: One Last Pass}
  Before submission, every table and figure needs:

  \vspace{6pt}

  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
    \item A title that states the \textbf{takeaway}, not the contents
    \item A caption you could read standalone and understand
    \item Units on every axis and every coefficient
    \item A note explaining the sample, standard errors, and any filters
    \item No raw variable names (``log\_earn\_2020'' $\to$ ``Log earnings, 2020\$'')
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{8pt}

  \textbf{Rule of thumb:} if a reader flipping through only to the figures could reconstruct your main result, your figures are doing their job.
\end{frame}

\section{Revision Sprint 2}

\begin{frame}{Sprint 2: Polish + Presentation Prep (50 min)}
  Pick a track based on where you have the most leverage:

  \vspace{8pt}

  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{\textcolor{usfgreen}{Paper Track}}
      \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
        \item Rewrite your intro's first paragraph
        \item Fix one table + one figure's labels/captions
        \item Read one section out loud; fix stumbles
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{\textcolor{usfgreen}{Slides Track}}
      \begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
        \item Draft your title slide + research question slide
        \item Build the 1--2 result slides that carry the talk
        \item Cut any slide you can't justify in a sentence
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}

  \vspace{10pt}

  Most of you should do slides track --- the paper gets one more week, but the talk needs a dry run today.
\end{frame}

\section{Presentation Dry Runs}
% ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\begin{frame}{Dry Run Pairs (30 min)}
  Pair up. Each person gets \textbf{10 minutes to talk}, then 5 minutes of feedback.

  \vspace{8pt}

  \textbf{Ground rules:}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
    \item Stand up. Talk to your partner, not the screen.
    \item Partner times you. If you hit 10 min, they stop you.
    \item Even if your slides aren't done, talk through the structure.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{8pt}

  \textbf{Feedback focus (for the listener):}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
    \item Could you state the research question after hearing the talk?
    \item What was the clearest moment? The most confusing?
    \item Was there a slide you'd cut?
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Presentation Logistics (May 7 \& May 14)}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{2pt}
    \item \textbf{10 minutes of talk + 5 minutes Q\&A.} Hard cap on the 10.
    \item Slides due uploaded to Canvas \textbf{the night before} your presentation.
    \item Bring your laptop as a backup, but we'll run off the room machine.
    \item First presenters go 4:40; last presenter ends by 8:00.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{8pt}

  \textbf{What a good 10-minute talk looks like:}
  \begin{itemize}\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
    \item 1 min: question + why it matters
    \item 2 min: data + empirical strategy
    \item 5 min: main results (with figures)
    \item 1 min: robustness / caveats
    \item 1 min: takeaway
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
